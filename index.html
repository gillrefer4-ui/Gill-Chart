Lesson 01 · Forex and XAUUSD

Forex is the global, decentralized market for trading currencies and metals against each other, open 24 hours a day, five days a week, through brokers such as MT5 rather than one central exchange. Instruments are quoted BASE/QUOTE: the price is how much quote currency buys one unit of base currency. In EUR/USD, EUR is the base and USD is the quote. XAUUSD is a metal pair, not a currency pair: its price is the USD amount for one troy ounce of gold.

Gill Chart reports an image-based chart reading; it does not place or manage orders. A single chart image cannot confirm higher-timeframe structure. Unclear readings or missing required context mean NO TRADE; predictions are never certain.

  • Bid is the price the broker will buy from you; ask is the price the broker will sell to you. Spread is ask minus bid and is the cost of entering; gold's spread is wider than EURUSD's.
  • Gill Chart's XAUUSD convention: 1.00 price move = 100 points; 0.10 = 10 points; 0.01 = 1 point.
  • Gill Chart's lot convention: 1.00 lot = 100 ounces; 0.01 lot = 1 ounce. Verify contract specifications with the broker.
  • Leverage lets a smaller balance control a larger position and increases both risk and potential return. Margin is held while a trade is open.
  • BUY/LONG expects price to rise; SELL/SHORT expects it to fall. Entry is where the trade starts. A stop loss (SL) automatically closes at a loss to protect the account and must always be set; take profit (TP) closes at the intended profit.
  • Risk-to-reward compares risk distance with reward distance. For example, risking 3.00 to target 6.00 gives RR 1:2.
  • Sydney is typically quiet/ranging; Tokyo/Asian is low-to-medium volatility and gold often ranges. Prioritize London (approximately 10:00–19:00 EAT) and New York (approximately 15:00–00:00 EAT); New York is typically most volatile for XAUUSD.
  • Banks and institutions have the largest volume, followed by hedge funds, central banks (including through rates and news), and retail traders. Follow institutional movement rather than fight it.
Lesson 02 · Candlesticks and chart reading

For this lesson, read only the candlesticks, current-price horizontal line, Y-axis (price), and X-axis (time). Ignore indicators, drawings, buttons, popups, account details, and unrelated chart objects. An uploaded screenshot is a single snapshot, so it cannot track a live price line over time.

  • The Y-axis on the right is price, increasing upward; the X-axis along the bottom is time, moving left to right. Each candle is one period with Open (start), High (top wick), Low (bottom wick), and Close (end). Never guess prices: read them against the nearest Y-axis labels and interpolate between those labels. Zoom changes label spacing, not the candle's price.
  • The body is between Open and Close: body size = |Close − Open|. The upper wick is High − max(Open, Close); the lower wick is min(Open, Close) − Low. Large bodies indicate stronger movement; small bodies indicate weaker movement or indecision. As a consistent visual heuristic, wicks longer than 10% of the full candle range suggest rejection and may mark liquidity sweeps; shorter or absent wicks suggest acceptance. Do not ignore gold wicks.
  • Close above Open is bullish (often green/white); Close below Open is bearish (often red/black). A doji is indecision: Gill Chart classifies a body no larger than 10% of the candle's full High-to-Low range as a doji; do not enter on a doji alone. Use “bearish,” never “berry.”
  • Read current price where the dashed horizontal price line meets the Y-axis. The rightmost candle is the most recent and may still be forming; report the last closed candle. A screenshot cannot show whether the live line is moving up or down or be tracked every second.
  • Identify timeframe from X-axis date/time labels, not the toolbar: months/years = MN1; weekly dates = W1; daily dates = D1; day plus 4-hour steps = H4; hourly steps = H1; 30-, 15-, 5-, or 1-minute steps = M30/M15/M5/M1. Label spacing can vary with zoom, but candle time intervals remain fixed.
  • Higher timeframes (MN1/W1/D1/H4) show trend and major structure; H1/M30/M15 show structure and entry zones; M5/M1 are for timing and confirmation, never bias alone. Higher-timeframe direction controls lower-timeframe decisions.

For a readable chart image, report: Timeframe; Current Price; Last Closed Candle (O, H, L, C, Type); Body Size; Upper Wick Size; Lower Wick Size; Interpretation (buyers/sellers, rejection, or indecision). Mark any unclear reading unavailable instead of guessing.

Lesson 03 · Market structure

Read structure before considering a trade. Use swing highs and lows, not every small candle move: higher highs with higher lows indicate an uptrend; lower highs with lower lows indicate a downtrend. When neither sequence is clear, treat it as range/unclear and stay out.

  • Compare four swing highs and four swing lows in chronological order. A valid trend requires each of the three high-to-high and low-to-low comparisons to confirm the same direction. Mixed sequences are not a confirmed trend.
  • Classify the higher timeframe first, then the chart timeframe. A lower-timeframe trade against the higher-timeframe bias is counter-trend and riskier.
  • Impulse is the strong move in the trend direction; correction is the smaller move against it. Do not chase an extended impulse. The phase must be identified from the chart.
  • BOS is only confirmed when a fully closed candle closes beyond the prior swing high/low in the trend direction. CHoCH is a close beyond the prior higher low in an uptrend or lower high in a downtrend. A wick alone does not count.
  • With-trend trades require structure agreement and a confirming BOS after a correction. Counter-trend trades require a confirming BOS, are lower probability, and call for early profit-taking and break-even protection. A range, unclear structure, or missing confirmation means NO TRADE.
  • Use the next swing high/low as TP1; consider TP2 only after continuation BOS and higher-timeframe agreement. Do not extend a lower-timeframe move past the higher-timeframe swing level.

The image scan estimates swing points from visible candle shapes. Image recognition can be wrong; verify its structure and every marked level before considering a trade.
