Lesson 01 · Forex and XAUUSD

Forex is the global, decentralized market for trading currencies and metals against each other, open 24 hours a day, five days a week, through brokers such as MT5 rather than one central exchange. Instruments are quoted BASE/QUOTE: the price is how much quote currency buys one unit of base currency. In EUR/USD, EUR is the base and USD is the quote. XAUUSD is a metal pair, not a currency pair: its price is the USD amount for one troy ounce of gold.

Gill Chart does not trade. It analyzes price structure and, only when a setup is clear, reports direction, entry, stop loss, take profit, and reasoning. Low probability means NO TRADE; predictions are never certain.

  • Bid is the price the broker will buy from you; ask is the price the broker will sell to you. Spread is ask minus bid and is the cost of entering; gold's spread is wider than EURUSD's.
  • Gill Chart's XAUUSD convention: 1.00 price move = 100 points; 0.10 = 10 points; 0.01 = 1 point.
  • Gill Chart's lot convention: 1.00 lot = 100 ounces; 0.01 lot = 1 ounce. Verify contract specifications with the broker.
  • Leverage lets a smaller balance control a larger position and increases both risk and potential return. Margin is held while a trade is open.
  • BUY/LONG expects price to rise; SELL/SHORT expects it to fall. Entry is where the trade starts. A stop loss (SL) automatically closes at a loss to protect the account and must always be set; take profit (TP) closes at the intended profit.
  • Risk-to-reward compares risk distance with reward distance. For example, risking 3.00 to target 6.00 gives RR 1:2.
  • Sydney is typically quiet/ranging; Tokyo/Asian is low-to-medium volatility and gold often ranges. Prioritize London (approximately 10:00–19:00 EAT) and New York (approximately 15:00–00:00 EAT); New York is typically most volatile for XAUUSD.
  • Banks and institutions have the largest volume, followed by hedge funds, central banks (including through rates and news), and retail traders. Follow institutional movement rather than fight it.
Lesson 02 · Candlesticks and chart reading

Read only the candlesticks, current-price horizontal line, Y-axis (price), and X-axis (time). Ignore indicators, buttons, popups, account details, and unrelated chart objects.

  • Each candle represents one time period and has Open (start), High (highest), Low (lowest), and Close (end). Its body size is |Close − Open|; upper wick is High − max(Open, Close); lower wick is min(Open, Close) − Low. Large bodies show strong movement; small bodies show weakness or indecision.
  • Close above Open is bullish (green/white); Close below Open is bearish (red/black); a near-equal open and close is a doji/indecision. A doji alone is not an entry. An upper wick shows rejection from higher prices/sellers pushing down; a lower wick shows rejection from lower prices/buyers pushing up. Long wicks can mark rejection or liquidity sweeps; short or absent wicks can show acceptance.
  • Read current price where the dashed live-price line meets the Y-axis. Price rises up the Y-axis. Use the nearest price labels to interpolate; zoom changes label spacing, not price values.
  • Use X-axis date/time spacing to identify timeframe, not the toolbar: monthly labels indicate MN1, weekly date steps W1, daily labels D1, day-plus-hour H4/H1, and hour/minute steps M30/M15/M5/M1. The rightmost candle is current/most recent; candle spacing in time stays fixed for a timeframe regardless of zoom.
  • Higher timeframes (MN1/W1/D1/H4) set trend and major structure; H1/M30/M15 inform structure and entries; M5/M1 are for timing and confirmation, not bias alone. Higher timeframe structure controls lower timeframe decisions.

Candle-reading worksheet

Read the values from the chart yourself, using the X-axis for timeframe and the current-price line against the Y-axis. The worksheet calculates candle type and wick/body sizes; it does not inspect the image.

Enter the last fully closed candle. A doji threshold is not specified in the lessons, so only an exactly equal Open and Close is automatically labeled doji; near-equal candles need visual judgment. No entry, stop-loss, or take-profit signal is generated by this worksheet.
